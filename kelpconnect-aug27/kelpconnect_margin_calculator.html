Where these numbers come from

Verified figures, sourced ranges, and your own inputs

Verified. The $500/day removal rate is the rate California paid commercial divers under state-funded purple urchin removal at Mendocino sites (2020–22), where the urchins were landed for compost. The GSI figures are species-matched: purple urchins taken from barrens showed a gonad index of roughly 3.5%, rising to about 14.9% after ten weeks on nutrient-enriched giant kelp. Marketable-quality uni is generally characterized by a GSI above 12%.

Sourced ranges. Ex-vessel figures come from CDFW landings receipts and PacFIN data reported in trade press; per-pound values are calculated from published pounds and dollar values rather than quoted directly. Wholesale and retail bands come from commercial market-data aggregators and direct vendor listings — indicative, not authoritative. No California-specific purple urchin roe wholesale price was found; the wholesale band covers sea urchin generally.

Your inputs. Ranching cost, survival rate, processing, freight, and channel margins remain editable placeholders with no sourcing behind them. Replace them with your own numbers before drawing conclusions.

A caveat on yield. Gonad index as a measurement has been challenged in the literature for resting on assumptions about how gonad size scales with body size. Treat the GSI figures as study-condition results rather than guaranteed commercial yields; grow-out results vary by feed, temperature, season, and stock condition.

A caveat on the wild-direct price. The default $14/lb sits mid-band for wholesale uni, which may be generous for roe taken directly from barrens at a 3.5% gonad index — barren urchins are described in the literature as having no marketable gonads at all. If anything, this default flatters the wild-direct scenario.
