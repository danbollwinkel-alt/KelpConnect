Where this comes from

KelpConnect doesn't monitor kelp, fund restoration, or put divers in the water. Other people do that, and this platform runs on their data and their labor. This page says who they are.

None of these organizations have partnered with, endorsed, or reviewed KelpConnect. They appear here because their work is real, publicly available, and in several cases because the Certified Restorative assessment would not function without data they publish. Listing is credit, not affiliation.

“A successful restoration program must be grounded in self-sustaining economic models.”

The data behind the score

Every input to a Certified Restorative assessment traces back to one of these. If you want to check our work, start here rather than with us.

KelpWatch Open data

Near-real-time satellite monitoring of California kelp canopy, built by The Nature Conservancy, Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, UCLA, and UC Santa Barbara, with Ocean Protection Council funding. Open to anyone.

Locality and restoration-zone alignment scoring

CDFW — Kelp Restoration and Management Plan State agency

California's in-development statewide plan for kelp restoration and management, with an active community working group. The most current framework for what counts as a restoration priority area.

Restoration priority zones; harvest permitting context

California Ocean Protection Council State agency

Released the 2021 Kelp Action Plan, funds the statewide Kelp Recovery Research Program, and has committed to restoring 2,000 acres of degraded kelp forest by 2030.

The 2,000-acre commitment; removal program funding records

California Sea Grant — Kelp Research Research program

Co-administers the Kelp Recovery Research Program with OPC and CDFW, including work on land-based echinoculture — the science behind urchin ranching.

Roe yield and grow-out assumptions in the margin calculator

Who does the work in the water

Restoration on this coast is done by a small number of organizations working with commercial and volunteer divers. Several are plausible suppliers on this platform; none has agreed to be.

Noyo Center for Marine Science — Help the Kelp Fort Bragg

Coordinates urchin removal on the Mendocino coast, runs public education programming, and is developing a regenerative aquaculture wet lab. Has argued publicly that restoration needs self-sustaining economic models rather than indefinite grant funding.

The argument this whole platform is built on

Reef Check California — Kelp Forest Program Monitoring & restoration

Runs the largest scuba-based citizen science monitoring program on the coast and led restoration at Noyo Bay and Albion Cove with CDFW, OPC, The Nature Conservancy, and Noyo Center. In the first month at Noyo Bay, 13 commercial divers cleared roughly six acres and landed 21,276 lb of purple urchin.

Urchin density monitoring protocols; restoration-contribution evidence

The Bay Foundation — Southern California kelp restoration Southern California

Coordinates urchin barren clearing in southern California with volunteer and commercial divers. Received up to $707,000 from OPC in March 2026 for Channel Islands kelp restoration. Linked here via NOAA's account of the work, as we haven't independently confirmed their current program page.

Southern California restoration site context

Learn more

If you want to understand the kelp crisis rather than transact in it, these are better starting points than anything we've written.

The Mysterious World of Bull Kelp Science communication

An illustrated web project by artist Josie Iselin covering kelp species, regional stories, restoration strategies, and aquaculture. The most approachable introduction to why any of this matters.

Monterey Bay Aquarium — Kelp Forest Resilience & Recovery Research

Studies why kelp forests become urchin barrens and when they recover, including the role of urchin behavior and sea otter presence, with CDFW and Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary.

Seafood Watch Rating system

The established sustainable seafood rating system, with a published, peer-reviewed scoring methodology. Certified Restorative is designed to complement it, not replace it — Seafood Watch scores harm avoidance, which is a different question from restoration contribution.

The benchmark our own methodology was built against

Kelp Forest Alliance Global database

A searchable record of kelp restoration projects worldwide, including the Mendocino sites. Useful for seeing how California's situation compares to kelp loss elsewhere.
